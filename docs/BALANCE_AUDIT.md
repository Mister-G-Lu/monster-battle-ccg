# Monster Battle CCG Balance Audit

Generated by `python3 scripts/analyze_balance.py --markdown`.

## Principles used

Research note: the healthiest CCG pattern I found is to separate rarity from raw power: make broadly useful staples common, and make rare cards more specialized/complex rather than simply stronger. See Game Studies, "Rarity and Power: Balance in Collectible Object Games" (https://gamestudies.org/1001/articles/ham). Mobile/F2P monetization guidance says the same thing in practical terms: avoid paid combat advantages, keep fairness, and offer non-paying progression paths.

- Rarity should buy specialization, deck-building texture, and timing decisions, not a direct stat upgrade.
- Common and Rare cards should remain efficient, readable staples.
- Legendary cards can be splashy or multi-modal, but their rate must stay inside the same cost budget.
- Tutorial/boss cards must be flagged out of random rewards and prototype card pools.

## Honest evaluation

- The collectible level-1 monster set is not wildly pay-to-win on raw HP: Common/Rare averages sit close together at costs 1-4.
- Legendary averages do jump at cost 3, so Legendary designs should be reviewed card-by-card and pushed toward conditional/choice-heavy effects instead of more HP plus more attack.
- The worst apparent power-creep example is actually the `flags=0` tutorial card Vibranium Armor; Gerbip king has been moved out of the 1-cost slot and remains non-collectible. The bug was exposure: the web prototype treated special cards as normal random cards.
- The production offline reward pool is already curated; keep it that way and do not switch rewards to sample from all cards without applying `flags=1` and budget checks.

## Recommended next design pass

1. For each Legendary that exceeds its same-cost Common/Rare budget, remove 1-2 HP or raise cost before adding any new abilities.
2. Prefer legendary hooks like delayed bonuses, positional trade-offs, once-per-battle choices, or synergy enablers; avoid unconditional `better body + better attack` packages.
3. Keep commons as efficient baseline bodies with one obvious job. They should be boring-good, not obsolete.
4. Add this audit to CI once the final budget formula is agreed by design.
